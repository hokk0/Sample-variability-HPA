# Sample-variability-HPA
The Human Protein Atlas (HPA) is a widely used resource for protein expression data, providing immunohistochemical annotations across different tissues with thousands of genes and antibodies. Despite its widespread use, its semi-quantitative descriptors: expression level, staining intensity and fraction of positively stained cells have not been systematically evaluated. This study assessed variability and agreement within HPA annotations using computational analysis in R across 4.9 million observations.  
Agreement between descriptors was evaluated using Quadratic Weighted Kappa (QWK), with substantial agreement observed between expression score and quantity (κ = 0.75) and between intensity and quantity (κ = 0.72). However, heatmap and stratified analyses revealed that disagreement is concentrated in intermediate categories, particularly “weak” staining intensity, which showed minimal agreement (3.1%) compared to high agreement for “negative” entries (92.5%). Further analysis demonstrated that disagreement does not cluster by tissue type but varies markedly across antibodies, with several exhibiting disagreement rates exceeding 80%. This suggests that technical factors, particularly antibody-specific variability, are a primary source of inconsistency rather than biological heterogeneity.
Overall, while the HPA demonstrates broad internal consistency, its semi-quantitative annotation system introduces uncertainty in low-signal regions, highlighting the need for cautious interpretation and integration of additional quality metrics in downstream analyses.
